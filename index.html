01 The craft

What leaves this table.

Four kinds of work. If yours is stranger than these, bring it anyway.

01 The city suit Jackets and trousers for the week you actually have.

Jackets and trousers for work, dinners, and the days between them. Canvas where you want shape. Ease where you actually move. The shoulder is cut from your posture, including the side you carry a bag on.

On the ticket: cloth chosen in the room, two fittings, working buttonholes.

02 Ceremony Sherwani, bandhgala, the wedding set.

Sherwanis, bandhgalas, and wedding sets for the hours you will really stand. We check the hem with your shoes. Embroidery only if you want the cloth to speak first.

On the ticket: a fitting in the real cloth, room for a long evening, family pieces welcome as reference.

03 Shirts Collars cut for your neck, not a chart.

A collar shaped to your neck, a cuff that meets without a gap, and a length that either stays tucked or is cut not to. The first shirt teaches us. The second one already knows.

On the ticket: your collar, your cuff, the cloth you reach for most.

04 The quiet fix Hems, shoulders, linings, second lives.

Hems, waists, shoulders, linings. We alter clothes we did not make. Bring the garment, not a photograph of the problem.

On the ticket: most small jobs return the same week. A relining takes longer, and we will say so.

02 The method

Five marks before it is yours.

  1. 01

    Conversation

    What you already wear. What pulls. What you wish sat quieter. A jacket you love is welcome in the room.

  2. 02

    Measure

    Shoulder, neck, posture, the way you stand when you forget you are being looked at. It goes in the book.

  3. 03

    Cloth

    You touch it. We talk weight, season, and how it will age after a year of real use.

  4. 04

    Basted fitting

    Held together with white thread, so we can still change our minds. This is where the garment becomes honest.

  5. 05

    The garment

    Pressed, buttoned, and not called finished until it sits. If it fights you, it stays on the table.

04 The house

Pin first.
Promise later.

Mars Tailors is a fitting room with a cutting table in it, not a floor of sizes. The name is on the door. The work is slower than the name sounds.

You will stand. We will pin. Nothing is cut until the cloth has already told the truth on your body.

Bring the jacket that almost works. We learn more from that than from a catalogue.

05 The table

What we fixed.

Illustrations of the work, written the way we talk about it in the room. Not reviews.

A city suit

The rolled shoulder

He came in wearing a jacket that fought his right shoulder. We let that side out, shortened the lapel roll, and the collar met his neck.

A wedding sherwani

The long evening

A wedding that would not end at dinner. Lighter canvas, a lining that breathes, and the hem checked in the shoes he planned to dance in.

An alteration

The old jacket

Ten years old. Good cloth, tired lining. We replaced the lining, eased the waist, and left the worn cuff buttons where his hand expected them.
